# Seed Data Knowledge Base for Examination Management & Question Paper Generation Using TCET Mumbai

## 1. Institution Profile

### 1.1 Core identity
Thakur College of Engineering & Technology (TCET) is an autonomous engineering college located at A‑Block, Thakur Educational Campus, Shyamnarayan Thakur Marg, Thakur Village, Kandivali (East), Mumbai 400101.[^1][^2]  
It is approved by AICTE and the Government of Maharashtra and is affiliated to the University of Mumbai as an autonomous college under the UGC graded autonomy scheme.[^2][^3]

TCET was established in the academic year 2001–02 to provide quality technical education and now offers 21 undergraduate, 11 postgraduate and 5 Ph.D. programmes in engineering and technology.[^2][^3]  
The institute is accredited by NAAC with an A grade and its eligible programmes are accredited by NBA for three years in multiple cycles; it is also ISO 9001:2015, 14001:2015 and 50001:2018 certified.[^4][^2]

### 1.2 Autonomy and schemes
TCET received autonomous status from the academic year 2019–20 initially for six branches of engineering (E&TC, Computer, IT, Electronics, Mechanical, Civil).[^5]  
Under autonomy, it introduced the Choice Based Credit Grading Scheme with Holistic Student Development (CBCGS‑H 2019) with a 25–75 internal–external pattern, later revised to CBCGS‑HME 2020 with a 40–60 pattern combining In‑Semester Examinations (ISE), Innovative Examinations (IE) and End‑Semester Examinations (ESE).[^5][^6]

For B.E. Computer Engineering, a full CBCGS‑H 2019 scheme for semesters I–VIII under TCET autonomy is documented, including program‑specific and holistic components.[^7]  
For newer B.Tech programmes such as Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning (AIML), CBCGS‑HME 2020 schemes are defined from semester I onward.[^8][^9]

## 2. Department Catalogue

### 2.1 Computer Engineering (COMP)
The Computer Engineering department started in AY 2001–02 and is permanently affiliated to the University of Mumbai since AY 2015–16.[^11]  
It has an intake of 180 students in first year plus 20 direct second‑year (lateral entry) students, with a 4‑year B.E. Computer Engineering, 4‑year B.E. CSE (Cyber Security), 2‑year M.E. Computer Engineering and Ph.D. Computer Engineering.[^11]

The department vision is "To become the department of national relevance in the field of Computer Engineering" and its mission emphasizes sound engineering knowledge, modern tools, global employability, leadership, ethics and social sensitivity.[^12][^7]  
The department lists Basic, Core, Advanced and Application‑based subject clusters, covering foundational CS theory, systems, networks, intelligent systems, security, big data and advanced software engineering.[^11]

Key quantitative attributes:

- Faculty strength: 26 (as per department highlights).[^11]  
- Labs: 6 UG labs, 1 PG lab, Ph.D. research centre.[^11]

## 3. Program Catalogue and Curriculum Schemes

### 3.1 Degree types and durations
All listed B.E. and B.Tech engineering programmes are 4‑year undergraduate programmes with 8 semesters.[^10][^7]  
M.E. programmes are 2‑year postgraduate programmes following a choice‑based semester and grading system, and Ph.D. programmes follow UGC norms.[^26][^7]

### 3.2 Curriculum schemes and patterns

#### 3.2.1 CBCGS‑H 2019 (B.E. COMP and other BE under early autonomy)
The Computer Engineering scheme under CBCGS‑H 2019 covers B.E. semesters I–VIII with program‑specific theory, lab and holistic student development components.[^7]  
The scheme is learner‑centric, starting with basic engineering sciences, mathematics and programming, followed by professional core courses, advanced electives and open electives, with PBL, ABL, RBL and employability skills integrated.[^7]

Credit structure for Computer Engineering (typical values from IT department OBE presentation, similar for COMP) indicates around 176 academic credits plus 30 holistic credits and 18 additional major/minor credits, reaching a grand total around 232 with optional extraordinary achievement credits.[^27]

#### 3.2.2 CBCGS‑HME 2020 (revised holistic and multidisciplinary scheme)
From AY 2021–22, TCET revised schemes to CBCGS‑HME 2020 with a 40:60 assessment pattern and explicit holistic and multidisciplinary education components.[^5]  
Under this scheme, most core courses have IA marks split into ISE and IE (20+20) and ESE 60 marks, with HME courses like Professional Skills, Employability Skills, PBL and ABL delivered in the first three weeks of each semester.[^7][^8]

AIML and other newer B.Tech programmes are fully designed under CBCGS‑HME 2020; Computer and IT are partially migrated in SE/TE/BE semesters.[^8][^9]

## 4. Master Subject Catalogue (Structure)

### 4.1 Subject entity structure
Based on the syllabi, each subject can be represented with these core fields:[^29][^23][^7][^8]

- subject_code (e.g., PCC‑CS301, BSC‑AIML401)
- subject_name (e.g., Data Structures, Statistical Decision Making)
- department (e.g., COMP, IT, AIML, HNS)
- program (e.g., B.E. COMP, B.Tech AIML)
- curriculum_scheme (CBCGS‑H 2019, CBCGS‑HME 2020, CBCGS‑HME 2023)
- academic_year_effective (e.g., 2021–22)
- semester_number (1–8)
- subject_type: one of
  - Basic Science (BSC)
  - Engineering Science Course (ESC)
  - Professional Core Course (PCC)
  - Professional Elective Course (PEC)
  - Open Elective Course (OEC)
  - Humanities and Social Science / HSMC
  - Mandatory/Non‑credit (MC)
  - Project/PROJ
  - Internship/SI
  - Activity Points/AP
  - Holistic/HSD/HME components (PS, PBL, ABL, RBL, ESD)
- lecture_hours_per_week
- tutorial_hours_per_week
- practical_hours_per_week
- contact_hours_per_week
- credits
- exam_scheme:
  - ia_total_marks, with ISE_marks, IE_marks
  - ese_marks
  - practical_oral_marks
  - term_work_marks
  - total_marks

The master subject catalogue should additionally include a normalized "canonical subject" concept for cross‑department reuse (e.g., Mathematics I, Programming for Problem Solving) so that core FE courses are not duplicated per department but referenced from HNS.[^23][^25]

## 7. Semester Progression and Academic Milestones

### 7.1 Generic progression
For all 4‑year BE/B.Tech engineering programmes, the progression is:

- Semester 1–2: FE (common basic science and engineering) conducted by HNS for all branches.[^18][^23]
- Semester 3–4: SE – branch‑specific core foundations, initial professional skills, first internships.
- Semester 5–6: TE – advanced core, professional electives, open electives, mini‑projects, internships, enhanced holistic skills.
- Semester 7–8: BE – specialization via high‑level electives, major capstone projects (Project I & II), open electives III & IV, placement‑linked internships.

### 7.2 Mini projects, PBL, internships, capstone
- Project Based Learning (PBL I–IV) is integrated from SE through TE in COMP and AIML; each PBL course expects mini‑projects with increasing complexity.[^7][^9]  
- Research Based Learning (RBL I–IV) appears in TE and BE semesters and is tied to research projects, publications, patents or technical competitions.[^7][^8]
- Summer Internship modules (SI‑xxxx) accumulate 120–240 hours of work across semesters III–VI, with credits awarded in a later semester’s grade card (often at the end of semester 6).[^29][^8][^28]
- BE/Final year Project I & II (PROJ‑xxxx) form the capstone, usually with 6 credits in semester VII and 6 credits in semester VIII in both COMP and AIML, with term work and practical/oral evaluation.[^29][^8]

Prerequisites are usually conceptual rather than explicitly formalized; for example, Data Structures is a prerequisite to Algorithms, which in turn precedes Machine Learning and advanced topics.[^7][^9]  
In seed data, prerequisite chains can be modeled at course level (e.g., PCC‑CS301 → PCC‑CS401 → PCC‑CS501) based on syllabus sequencing.


## 8. Examination Structure

### 8.1 Components and terminology
TCET’s exam cell describes the evolution of evaluation under autonomy:[^5][^29][^6]

Key components:
- IA – Internal Assessment (In‑Semester Assessment)
- ISE – In‑Semester Examination (unit tests, usually 1 or 1.5 hours)
- IE – Innovative Examination (project/assignment based)
- ESE – End‑Semester Examination (theory exam)
- PR – Practical Examination
- OR – Oral Examination
- TW – Term Work (continuous lab/project evaluation)
- AC – Activity evaluation (for HME/HSD components)

### 8.2 Mark patterns
Two main patterns appear:

1. CBCGS‑H 2019 (early autonomy):
   - Theory: IA 25 marks, ESE 75 marks, paper duration 3 hours.[^29][^23]

2. CBCGS‑HME 2020 (revised):
   - Theory (100 marks): IA 40 marks (ISE 20 + IE 20), ESE 60 marks, paper duration 2 hours.[^6][^7][^8]
   - Some courses with 50 marks theory use scaled versions (20+10+20 or similar).[^8]

Holistic courses (Professional Skills, Employability Skills, PBL, ABL, RBL) often have evaluation split across presentation, report and term work marks (e.g., 50+25 for total 75, or 50 for ESD based on training and placement cell parameters).[^29][^9]

### 8.3 Continuous assessment and CO/PO mapping
The IT NBA document describes the outcome‑based assessment process:[^27]

- Direct tools: ISA/ISE, formational assessments, practical evaluation, ESE, BE projects.
- Indirect tools: course surveys, exit surveys, alumni surveys, holistic development activities.
- Final attainment: 80% direct + 20% indirect.

Question papers are set using Bloom’s taxonomy alignment and CO‑PO articulation matrices; CO attainment is measured with levels 1–3 based on percentage achievement.[^27]  
This is relevant because EMQPGS question paper generation must respect CO coverage, Bloom’s levels and weightage distribution.

### 8.4 Supplementary and transparency mechanisms
The exam cell notes:
- Blind selection of a question paper from a set of three prepared papers.
- Open house facility where students can view answer books and raise grievances.
- Supplementary theory examinations for unsuccessful students within a month of result declaration.
- Institute‑prepared grade cards with barcode, hologram, etc.[^5]

- Winter break: 25 December to 1 January (practice/doubt solving/preparatory leave).
- ESE oral/practical: 3–10 January.
- ESE theory: 12–22 January.
- Semester break: 23–26 January.[^33]

These patterns can be abstracted into an "AcademicYear" and "SemesterSession" seed model with date ranges but without hard‑coding specific calendar years.

## 12. Student Structure (Batches, Divisions, Rolls)

Public TCET sources do not explicitly describe batch naming patterns, division codes or roll number formats; however, standard Mumbai engineering practice and some hints can guide reasonable assumptions:[^27]

- Sanctioned intake is typically split into 2–3 divisions per year for high‑intake branches (e.g., 180 COMP seats → 3 divisions of 60, labeled A/B/C).
- Direct second‑year entries (DSE) may be grouped into an additional division or distributed across existing divisions.
- FE classes are common across branches but timetabled in groups; academic calendars and NBA data show SE/TE/BE headcounts (e.g., SE‑138, TE‑142, BE‑135 for IT).[^27]

Because no official roll conventions are published in sampled sources, the seed data should specify generic patterns rather than actual roll numbers, for example:

- Batch: BE‑COMP‑2022–26 (start year 2022, graduation 2026).
- Divisions: A, B, C.
- Roll number format: <Year><BranchCode><Division><Sequence> (to be defined by the EMQPGS schema, not by TCET data).

These should be clearly marked as inferred, not confirmed.


## 13. Academic Relationships for EMQPGS

### 13.1 Conceptual hierarchy
The academic relationships implied by TCET’s structure and the EMQPGS requirements can be represented as:

Institution (TCET)  
→ School/Faculty (Engineering & Technology)  
→ Department (e.g., COMP, IT, MECH, CIVIL, AIML)  
→ Program (e.g., B.E. Computer Engineering, B.Tech AIML)  
→ Curriculum Scheme (e.g., CBCGS‑H 2019, CBCGS‑HME 2020)  
→ Academic Year (e.g., intake cohort 2022–23 with scheme version)  
→ Semester (1–8)  
→ Subject (with type, credits, exam scheme)  
→ Class Offering (subject offered to batch and division in that semester)  
→ Faculty Assignment (course coordinator + instructors)  
→ Assessment Components (ISE, IE, ESE, PR, OR, TW, AC)  
→ Question Bank (per subject, per Bloom level, per unit/CO)  
→ Exam Instance (e.g., SE COMP Sem III ESE Dec 2025)  
→ Paper Set (3 variant question papers per exam, one selected blind)  
→ Exam Session (date, shift, room allocation)  
→ Result (per student, per subject, per exam cycle).

### 13.2 Integration of internships and holistic components
In addition to standard theory/lab subjects, the EMQPGS data model should treat HME/HSD courses as subjects with distinct assessment types:

- Professional Skills, PBL, ABL and RBL: evaluated via AC, presentation, report, TW but no ESE.
- Summer Internship: evaluated via report and presentation; credits awarded in later semesters; can be represented as separate "internship" subject types with non‑standard scheduling.
- Activity Points: non‑credit; require tracking of hours and events.

These should still map to question banks for formative assessments if the EMQPGS will manage quizzes or reflective assessments in such modules; otherwise they may be treated as non‑exam components.


## 14. Seed Data Recommendations

### 14.1 Scope for initial seed dataset
Given the volume of actual courses and departments at TCET, an initial seed dataset for development should focus on a realistically rich but bounded subset:

- Departments: COMP, IT, AIML, MECH, CIVIL, HNS.
- Programmes:
  - B.E. Computer Engineering.
  - B.E. Information Technology.
  - B.Tech AIML.
  - B.E. Mechanical Engineering (lightly modeled).
  - B.E. Civil Engineering (lightly modeled).
- Curriculum schemes:
  - CBCGS‑H 2019 for COMP/IT (SE–BE).
  - CBCGS‑HME 2020 for AIML (all semesters).
  - FE schema shared across branches via HNS.

### 14.2 Subjects and question banks
For each selected program:

- Model full subject lists for at least four consecutive semesters (e.g., SE and TE for COMP, semester I–IV for AIML, FE I–II for all branches).
- For each subject, categorize into:
  - Core theory.
  - Lab.
  - Professional elective.
  - Open elective.
  - Project.
  - Internship.
  - HME/HSD.
- For each subject, define exam components and mark distribution.
- Generate question bank placeholders per subject:
  - 5–10 units/modules with weightage.
  - 3–5 Blooms levels per unit.
  - 20–40 questions per unit for demonstration, tagged with CO and marks pattern (based on typical 25/75 or 40/60 splits).

Question bank content itself is not sourced from TCET and must be authored separately; the seed dataset should only specify the structure into which questions will be generated.

### 14.3 Faculty assignments
For seed realism while respecting available data:

- Assign 2–3 core theory courses per faculty per semester in their specialization area.
- Define coordinator roles (e.g., HOD as course coordinator for cornerstone subjects and for PBL/RBL).
- Mark these assignments as inferred.

Example mapping (inferred):

- Dr. Harshali Patil → Theory of Computer Science, Distributed Computing, Project I & II (COMP).[^30][^29]
- Dr. Rekha Sharma → Data Warehousing & Mining, Research Based Learning and PG‑level elective.[^30][^7]
- Dr. Rashmi Thakur → Employability Skill Development courses; Professional Skills and internships coordination.[^30][^27]

### 14.4 Academic years, batches and semesters
Seed records can define:

- AcademicYear entities (e.g., 2023–24, 2024–25) with associated semester sessions following the pattern in 9.1–9.2.
- Batches (e.g., BE‑COMP‑2023–27) with links to program and scheme.
- BatchSemester records mapping each batch to its semester progression (1–8) and scheduling.

### 14.5 Roles in EMQPGS
To support realistic workflows, seed data should include these roles:

- Department Coordinator (per department).
- Program Coordinator (per programme).
- Exam Cell Coordinator.
- Course Coordinator (per subject instance).
- Paper Setter.
- Moderator.
- Reviewer.
- Examiner (theory, practical, viva).

These can be mapped to faculty entities using many‑to‑many relations.

### 14.6 Exam cycles
Model exam cycles consistent with TCET’s terminologies:[^5][^29]

- ISE‑I (In‑Semester Examination I).
- ISE‑II.
- IE (Innovative Exam).
- ESE (Regular) – odd/even semester.
- Supplementary ESE (repeat exam).

Each ExamCycle entity should link to:

- Semester.
- Subjects.
- Assessment components and weightage.
- Question bank snapshot.
